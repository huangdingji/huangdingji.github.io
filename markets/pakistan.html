Customs and Import Documentation for Pakistan

HS code: HS code 3920.99 (plastic plates, sheets, film, foil and strip — of other plastics). Pakistan follows the Pakistan Customs Tariff, which is largely aligned with the global HS system.

Customs duty and charges: Customs duty on plastic products varies by HS code: typically 11% to 20% of CIF value for HS 3920.99 under the Pakistan Customs Tariff 2024-25. Additional charges include 5% to 10% sales tax, 3% additional customs duty, and 0.5% to 1% regulatory duty. Total landed cost adders can reach 30-40% of CIF, making price-sensitive buyers very keen on per-metre tape cost. Pakistan has signed a Free Trade Agreement (FTA) with China (CPFTA Phase-II) since 2019, which reduces duty on many Chinese-origin goods; for HS 3920.99, the preferential duty under CPFTA is significantly lower than the standard MFN duty.

Standard import documents:

  • Commercial invoice (original, signed, with HS code, country of origin, and Pakistan-specific details such as NTN and STRN of the importer).
  • Packing list with carton-level details.
  • Bill of Lading (B/L) for ocean freight, or Air Waybill (AWB) for air freight.
  • Certificate of Origin (COO) issued by CCPIT — for preferential duty under CPFTA, the COO must be in the agreed format and stamped by the authorised body.
  • Pakistan Customs Electronic Filing (WeBOC system) — the importer files the Goods Declaration (GD) electronically before the goods arrive.
  • For some product categories, a Pakistan Standards and Quality Control Authority (PSQCA) certificate is required, but PTFE thread seal tape is generally not in the mandatory PSQCA regulated list.

Ports of Entry and Air Cargo Gateways

Two main seaports: Karachi Port (the largest, handling about 60% of Pakistan's cargo, deep-water, gateway to Sindh and southern Punjab) and Port Muhammad Bin Qasim (Qasim Port, near Karachi, handles 35% of container traffic, gateway to the industrial areas of Punjab). Air freight through Jinnah International Airport (Karachi), Allama Iqbal International Airport (Lahore), and Islamabad International Airport for urgent re-orders. Land border crossings: Wagah (India-Pakistan, currently restricted for most goods), Chaman (Pakistan-Afghanistan, important for Afghanistan re-export), Torkham (Pakistan-Afghanistan, the main Khyber Pass crossing).

Local Market and Distribution Channels

Pakistan has a population of 240 million and a large, growing plumbing and hardware market. The largest cities are Karachi (Sindh, 16 million, the commercial capital and main port), Lahore (Punjab, 13 million, the cultural and industrial capital), Faisalabad (Punjab, 3 million, textile industry), Rawalpindi-Islamabad (4 million combined, the federal capital) and Peshawar (Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, gateway to Afghanistan). The construction sector is supported by government housing schemes (Naya Pakistan Housing), CPEC (China-Pakistan Economic Corridor) infrastructure projects, and a chronic urban housing shortage. Demand for plumbing tape is broad-based: residential construction, commercial projects, and the large LPG distribution network (Pakistan is one of the world's largest LPG importers). Yellow gas tape is in strong demand from LPG distributors, particularly in rural areas where LPG is the main cooking fuel. White plumbing tape is the volume SKU for the urban construction sector. Distribution channels are dominated by family-owned hardware wholesalers in major cities, with growing organised retail (Metro, Carrefour, Imtiaz). Re-export to Afghanistan is a significant channel, particularly for tape sold in Peshawar and Quetta.

Seasonal Demand and Planning Notes

Demand peaks before and during the cooler winter months (October-February) when plumbing and water heater installation work peaks. The monsoon season (July-August) can slow construction and import activity. Ramadan (date shifts yearly) briefly slows customs clearance. Plan container arrival at Karachi or Qasim 4-6 weeks before the winter peak season. Re-export to Afghanistan spikes in Q3-Q4 as Afghan construction projects ramp up before the harsh winter.

Frequently Asked Questions

What HS code applies to PTFE tape in Pakistan?

PTFE thread seal tape is classified under HS code 3920.99 (plastic plates, sheets, film, foil and strip — of other plastics). Under the China-Pakistan FTA (CPFTA) Phase-II, the preferential tariff for HS 3920.99 from China is significantly lower than the MFN tariff — make sure the Certificate of Origin is in the correct CPFTA format to claim the preference.

What is the total landed cost for PTFE tape in Pakistan?

Total landed cost adders (customs duty, sales tax, additional customs duty, regulatory duty) on HS 3920.99 from China can be 20-35% of CIF value under CPFTA preferential rates, or 30-40% under MFN rates. Always quote the landed cost to the Pakistani buyer, not just the FOB price, to avoid disputes later.

Which port is best for Pakistan imports?

Karachi Port and Port Muhammad Bin Qasim (Qasim) are the two main seaports, both within 50 km of each other. Qasim is preferred for containerised cargo because it has modern container terminals and less congestion. For Punjab destinations (Lahore, Faisalabad), Qasim is closer by road. For Sindh and southern Punjab, Karachi is fine.

Is L/C required for shipments to Pakistan?

L/C at sight is the standard payment method for first orders and for orders above USD 30,000. For established relationships, T/T 30/70 or open account 30 days is also common. Avoid 100% upfront payment from an unknown buyer. For L/C, choose a Pakistani bank confirmed by an international bank (HSBC, Standard Chartered) for additional security.

Can PTFE tape be re-exported to Afghanistan from Pakistan?

Yes. The Pakistan-Afghanistan border crossings at Torkham and Chaman are major re-export routes. PTFE tape imported through Karachi or Qasim and re-exported to Afghanistan is a well-established trade flow, particularly for tape sold in Peshawar, Quetta and the Afghan cities of Kabul, Kandahar, Herat and Mazar-i-Sharif. Pakistani importers in the border region often specialise in this re-export trade.
