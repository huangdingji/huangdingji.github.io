Start with the connection

Does this joint need a thread sealant?

First identify the joint’s sealing surface. A connection may seal at its threads, or at a separate gasket, O-ring, flare or seat. Do not add tape or compound just because a connection has threads. Follow the fitting manufacturer’s instructions for the particular connection.

If a thread sealant is specified, record the fitting material, thread type, media and operating conditions. These details make the choice more useful than a general “tape is better” or “paste is stronger” rule. Oatey’s tape and pipe-joint-compound guide also distinguishes connections that require thread sealing from those that seal elsewhere.

How PTFE tape differs from paste

PTFE thread seal tape is supplied as a film on a spool. For connections where it is specified, it is applied to the threads during assembly. The fitting instructions govern the amount, direction and tightening procedure. A roll’s width, actual length, thickness and density or net tape weight are procurement details, not a substitute for application approval.

“Thread sealant paste” covers different chemistries. Some pipe-joint compounds remain non-hardening; anaerobic sealants cure under their specified assembly conditions. A paste containing PTFE should not be assumed to behave like PTFE tape, or like every other paste.

When to choose tape, paste or a permitted combination

  • Choose tape when the fitting instructions allow it and the specified tape is suitable for the material, media and service conditions.
  • Choose paste when the specified compound is compatible with the joint and its assembly or cure requirements suit the work.
  • Use both only when permitted by the applicable fitting and sealant instructions. Obtain the specified sequence and quantity rather than adopting a generic method.

For gas, hot-water or other specified services, check the exact product documentation and applicable installation requirements. A yellow spool, white tape or a PTFE ingredient does not by itself establish approval. Do not infer a system pressure or temperature rating from the material name.

After selecting tape, compare the same specification

For a purchase comparison, state width, actual roll length, thickness and density or net tape weight, then confirm quantity and packaging. Check a sample against the agreed specification. A lower roll price does not establish a lower installed cost when product amount, assembly and testing differ.

Questions before assembly

Can I use PTFE tape and thread sealant paste together?

Only when the fitting and sealant manufacturers permit that combination for the specific joint and service. Follow their application sequence and quantity instructions; there is no universal wrap count or tape-plus-paste method.

Does all thread sealant paste need 24 hours to cure?

No. Non-hardening compounds and anaerobic curing sealants are different product types. Check the exact product data sheet for assembly, test pressure and return-to-service requirements rather than applying a fixed waiting time.

Which should I choose for gas lines or hot water?

Check the exact product rating, fitting material, media, operating conditions and applicable local requirements. Tape color or the word PTFE alone does not establish suitability. Neither tape nor paste is automatically the better choice for every system.

Can PTFE tape be used on plastic threads?

Follow the plastic fitting manufacturer’s instructions for the specified joint. Confirm whether tape or a compatible compound is permitted, together with the tightening instructions. Do not transfer instructions for metal fittings to plastic fittings.
