PTFE Tape Colors Explained: What Each Color Means

PTFE thread seal tape is manufactured in a range of colors, and although the color itself does not change the chemistry, color is a long-standing industry convention that signals the intended application. This article explains what each color means, where it should and should not be used, and how to plan a color mix for your market.

White PTFE Tape — General Purpose Plumbing

White is the most common PTFE tape color worldwide. It is the standard retail plumber tape used on cold water, hot water, faucets, valves, water heater connections and general household plumbing. The white color does not indicate a special grade: it is usually the same base PTFE resin as the colored versions, with no dye added. A 12mm × 10m × 0.075mm white roll at 0.3-0.4 g/cm³ density is the de-facto industry baseline SKU and the one most distributors order first.

White tape is not approved for gas lines in most regulated markets. In Saudi Arabia, the UAE, the United States, Canada and the European Union, gas fittings require a yellow gas-rated tape that meets a recognised standard such as EN 751-3, AS 4623-2008 or UPC. Using white tape on a gas line can void insurance and is a code violation in many jurisdictions.

Yellow PTFE Tape — Gas Line and Propane

Yellow is the universal signal for gas-rated PTFE tape. The yellow color is not decorative: it is a long-standing convention dating back to US gas code requirements from the 1960s, and it is now recognised worldwide. Yellow gas tape is usually higher density (0.8-1.2 g/cm³) and slightly thicker (0.1-0.2mm) than white plumbing tape, so it can handle the higher pressure and vibration found in natural gas and LPG systems.

For natural gas, propane, butane and LPG lines, yellow gas tape should be used. Some jurisdictions require both yellow tape and a liquid thread sealant paste; others accept the tape alone. For LPG cylinders and small portable appliances, the local standard may specify a thinner yellow tape. Always check the local gas code before specifying a yellow tape SKU.

Green PTFE Tape — Oxygen and Oil-Resistant Lines

Green PTFE tape is traditionally associated with oxygen service in medical, welding and industrial applications. Oxygen lines must be free of any hydrocarbon contamination, and green dye helps inspectors and technicians visually verify that the correct oxygen-rated tape has been used. Green tape is also used for certain oil-resistant applications and for some aviation fuel systems.

For industrial oxygen lines (hospital manifolds, welding gas lines, ozone generators), a clean green oxygen-rated PTFE tape is the industry standard. For oil and hydraulic lines at moderate pressure, green tape is often used as a visual cue, but the actual sealing performance comes from the same base PTFE resin used in white tape. If your application is purely oil-resistant, a higher-density white or yellow tape is usually a more cost-effective choice than green.

Red PTFE Tape — High Temperature and Industrial

Red PTFE tape signals high-temperature or industrial service. Red is widely used on steam lines, hot oil systems, heat exchangers and high-temperature fluid lines in factories and ship engine rooms. Red tape is usually a high-density grade (0.8-1.2 g/cm³) with thickness in the 0.1-0.2mm range, capable of continuous service at 200-260°C.

In some markets red is also used as an OEM private label color, so a red tape from one manufacturer may be high-temperature industrial grade while another supplier's red tape is a standard-density product with a red spool. Always confirm the specification (density, thickness, temperature rating) before assuming that red means high temperature. A red tape without a published density spec is most likely a marketing color, not a temperature-rated product.

Pink PTFE Tape — Potable Water and Food Grade

Pink PTFE tape indicates a food-grade or potable-water formulation. Pink tape is usually manufactured from virgin PTFE resin with no recycled content and no dye that could leach into drinking water, and it is often certified to NSF/ANSI 61, WRAS or a similar drinking water standard. Pink is the standard color for water lines in food processing, beverage production, pharmaceutical manufacturing and residential potable water systems in markets that require certification.

Where local code requires NSF or WRAS certification for any pipework carrying drinking water, pink tape is the safe default. In the United States, several state plumbing codes require NSF/ANSI 61 certification for any sealing material in contact with potable water. In the United Kingdom, WRAS approval is the equivalent standard. Pink is also used for rainwater harvesting lines and greywater recycling systems where the water may be reused for irrigation or toilet flushing.

Blue PTFE Tape — Industrial and Custom OEM

Blue PTFE tape is most often a custom OEM color used by distributors building a private label program. Blue is also used in some markets for industrial compressed air lines and for pneumatic systems, where the visual distinction helps maintenance crews identify the service type at a glance. Blue tape is otherwise chemically identical to white tape, with the same density and temperature rating.

For importers building a private label program, blue is a popular alternative to white because it differentiates the product on the shelf without requiring a food-grade or gas-grade certification. The cost premium for custom-color spool and tape is typically 3-5% over a standard white SKU at the same specification.

Black PTFE Tape — Heavy Duty and Engineering

Black PTFE tape signals heavy-duty or engineering service. Black is widely used in shipbuilding, oil and gas, chemical plants and industrial maintenance, where the dark color hides dirt and grease that would be obvious on a white roll. Black tape is often a high-density grade (1.0 g/cm³ or above) capable of withstanding higher pressure and more aggressive chemicals than standard white tape.

In ship engine rooms and offshore platforms, black PTFE tape is standard for hydraulic and seawater line connections. In chemical plants, black tape is often specified for mild acid and alkali service where standard white tape is acceptable but a higher-density grade is preferred. Black is also a popular OEM color for industrial product lines.

How to Plan a Color Mix for Your Market

For a new distributor or importer, the right color mix depends on the market. In the Middle East (Saudi Arabia, UAE, Iraq, Egypt), yellow gas tape typically accounts for 25-30% of volume and white plumbing tape for 60-70%, with the remaining 5-10% split across specialty colors. In the United States and Canada, white and yellow dominate, with pink gaining share as NSF/ANSI 61 requirements spread. In Europe, white and yellow are the two main SKUs, with pink as a specialist segment and green for medical oxygen.

A typical first container order might split as 60% white 12mm × 10m, 30% yellow 12mm × 10m (or 19mm for the gas program), and 10% specialty colors. After 2-3 cycles of sales data, the mix can be refined to match the actual demand of the channel.

FAQ

Does the color of PTFE tape change its chemical properties?

No. The PTFE base resin is the same regardless of color. The color is added with a small amount of dye (typically less than 0.1% by weight) and does not change the sealing performance. Color is a market convention that signals the intended application, not a chemical specification.

Can I use white PTFE tape on a gas line?

In most regulated markets, no. The standard requirement is a yellow gas-rated tape that meets a recognised standard such as EN 751-3, AS 4623-2008 or UPC. White plumbing tape is not approved for gas in Saudi Arabia, UAE, the United States, Canada or the European Union.

Is pink PTFE tape the same as NSF certified tape?

Pink is the visual convention for potable-water or food-grade tape, and most pink tape is manufactured to meet NSF/ANSI 61 or WRAS. However, the color alone does not guarantee certification — always verify the certificate number and the issuing lab before claiming compliance.

Which color is best for OEM private label?

Blue and black are the most common OEM private label colors because they are not associated with any specific certification (so no extra paperwork is needed) and they differentiate the product on the shelf. White can also be used but is harder to differentiate from generic plumber tape without a strong label design.
